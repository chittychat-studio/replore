Your data
Privacy

  
Replore has optional Google sign-in for Pro users and no user database, and stores nothing you type. Our host counts page views
    with Cloudflare Web Analytics, without cookies (see below).

  
    
    
  • Your search words go from your browser straight to GitHub (with your IP address) to run the search.
    • 
    
  • To sort repos by fit, your search words and the list of repos found go through our relevance check (our only
      server code) to an AI provider (Cloudflare Workers AI, or Groq when that is unavailable or over its daily limit, or Mistral AI only if we switch it on), which judges which repos fit.
      We don't store or log them; each provider handles them under its own terms
      (Cloudflare,
      Groq). If a check fails we record only the
      error type and time. If the check is unavailable you get the keyword results unchanged.
    • 
    
  • Daily AI checks: to share the free AI allowance, our server counts how many AI checks you ran today (5 a day, 20 if
      you are signed in; answers served from the cache are free). It keeps only that number, under a salted one-way
      fingerprint of your IP address (an IPv6 address by its /64 prefix) or, signed in, of your account id, never the address or id itself, and deletes every count at 00:00 UTC.
    • 
    
  • Meaning-based search (only when switched on): the same check asks an AI model what your words mean and looks
      them up in an index of public repo names and descriptions (Cloudflare Vectorize). Every repo found that way is checked
      live on GitHub before you see it.
    • 
    
  • Where repo data comes from: the GitHub API. Replore is not affiliated with or endorsed by GitHub. GitHub® is a trademark of GitHub, Inc.
    • 
    
  • Saved for an hour: your browser keeps your last 10 searches and their results on this device for one hour,
      so repeating one is instant. They never leave your device; the next search after an hour runs fresh. For identical
      searches, our relevance check also reuses the AI's answer for an hour; it keeps only a fingerprint of the search and which
      repos don't fit, never your words.
    • 
    
  • Bot check: Cloudflare Turnstile is on: your browser loads it from Cloudflare when you search, and
      it checks that you're a person before the AI check runs. It usually needs no click. Cloudflare handles it under the
      same privacy policy.
    • 
    
  • When the page loads, only Cloudflare (our host) sees your IP address; loading the page doesn't reveal what
      you search (the relevance check above does send your search words to Cloudflare). Fonts and
      scripts come from this site, not from Google or other CDNs; the only outside scripts are Cloudflare's bot check
      (above) and page-view counter (below). Searches are never put in the page address.
    • 
    
  • Page-view counts: our host runs Cloudflare Web Analytics on this site, a cookieless, privacy-first
      page-view counter run by Cloudflare and loaded from static.cloudflareinsights.com. It records the page
      address (your searches are never in it), the referring site, your browser, operating system and device type, your
      country and how fast the page loaded. It sets no cookie, stores nothing on your device, never sees what you type and
      gives us only totals. Cloudflare handles it under its
      privacy policy.
    • 
    
  • On your device only: your last search, results and filters, so a refresh brings them back. Use the clear button in the
      search bar, or the Replore logo, to delete them.
    • 
    
  • A Replore Pro key, only if you save one: it stays in this browser and goes only with your relevance checks
      to our function, which asks Stripe (who sells Pro) whether its subscription is active, under Stripe's privacy policy. We never
      keep it or log it, and it is never put in the page address or sent with feedback. Remove key, or clear this site's
      data, to delete it.
    • 
    
  • Optional Google sign-in (for Pro users, only if you use it): Firebase Authentication, run by Google, keeps your
      user id, Google email address, name, profile photo URL, the provider (google.com) and when you signed up and last signed in,
      plus a Pro link (whether Pro is on, your Stripe subscription id and when we last asked Stripe). Your browser keeps the
      sign-in token in IndexedDB and a replore.auth hint until you sign out. Payment details are held only by Stripe.
      Delete my account removes it all at once; it does not cancel Stripe. Signing in loads Google's sign-in from
      accounts.google.com only when you press Sign in with Google; Google may use its own cookies there. Everything else works signed out.
    • 
    
  • Uploaded plans are read in your browser and never uploaded; only short feature phrases are searched on GitHub.
    • 
    
  • Open in Claude or Open in ChatGPT sends the build-plan prompt to your own claude.ai or chatgpt.com
      account, only when you click it. Open in Gemini copies it and opens gemini.google.com; you paste it.
    • 
    
  • Look inside links in a card's Details (Diagram, Wiki, Code, and Also: Digest, Edit) open gitdiagram.com, gitingest.com, deepwiki.com, github1s.com or
      github.dev at the repo's public name, only when you click one; your searches are never sent.
    • 
    
  • Safe to build on? on a card, only when you open it: your browser asks GitHub's API about that repo, and our
      trust check sends the repo's public name and language to OpenSSF Scorecard and Google's deps.dev (vulnerability data
      from OSV.dev), without your IP address or your searches.
    • 
    
  • Feedback you send (title, type, message, and your email only if you add it) goes through
      Web3Forms (api.web3forms.com), an email-forwarding service, to hello@cardzo.co.in. Nothing is
      sent until you press Send.
    • 
    
  • No cookies, ads or tracking pixels from Replore.

How ranking works
About the results
   
  
Results are ranked by fit: how well a repo matches your words, how popular it is (stars)
    and how freely you can use its licence. So the top pick is relevant, popular and free to use, not just the
    most-starred.

  
How Leo searches: he splits what you describe into separate needs and
    searches GitHub for each, specific needs first. An AI check then reads the top 30 and moves repos that don't fit to
    the end, under "Less likely to fit". If the check is unavailable you get the keyword results unchanged. A search
    you repeat within an hour comes back instantly from your own browser.

  
On each card: when the code last changed, the licence in plain words
    (free for commercial use or not), stars, the language and whether it's a starter template. The filters on the left
    and the sort menu only narrow and reorder what was found; they never search again. Details shows why it
    fits, Diagram, Wiki and Code to look inside, and Is it safe to build on? (OpenSSF Scorecard, known
    vulnerabilities, releases), which is for information and never changes the ranking.

  
The 
    goes further: your own AI writes the searches, judges every result and reads a whole plan feature by feature.

  
Limits: results are GitHub search matches, not a scan of every
    repository. Stars measure popularity, not quality, and can be bought. The licence verdict comes from the licence
    only: a plain summary, not legal advice. Some projects also sell a paid hosted version or add-ons.

  
Hybrid search: repos are ranked by your words and by what you mean, so "voice notes" also finds "speech-to-text". Repos our index finds that the word search missed are checked on GitHub before you see them.
